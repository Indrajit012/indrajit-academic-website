Magnetic field structure in IC 342

Paul, I., Gogoi, A. S., Kashyap, R. V., Ghosh, T., Beck, R., Chamandy, L., Sinha, S., Shukurov, A.

Manuscript in preparation

All publications
Overview

The "Hidden Galaxy," unveiled in radio

IC 342 is a nearby, low-inclination, grand-design spiral galaxy that — together with the elliptical galaxy Maffei 1 — dominates one of the galaxy groups closest to our own Local Group. Despite lying among the nearest large spirals to the Milky Way, it is heavily dimmed and reddened by dust in our own Galactic plane, earning it the nickname the "Hidden Galaxy." Its near face-on orientation and large angular extent, however, make it one of the best-resolved nearby galaxies for studying the fine structure of a large-scale galactic magnetic field.

This paper extends the Bayesian Fourier-mode framework developed for M31 to IC 342, combining VLA and Effelsberg radio polarisation data to trace the galaxy's regular magnetic field continuously from its nuclear bar out to the edge of its star-forming disk.

IC 342 is classified as an SAB(rs)cd-type spiral, with a flat rotation curve of ~170 km/s out to at least 42 kpc. Its near face-on geometry gives an unusually clean view of the galaxy's spiral structure, from its bar-fed nucleus to its outer star-forming disk.

Observational data

Two telescopes, matched to two regions

The central, bar-fed nucleus and the extended outer disk demand different angular resolutions — so this analysis pairs high-resolution VLA data for the core with wide-field Effelsberg data for the disk, spanning four wavelengths in total.

Method

Extending the Bayesian framework to a new galaxy

01

Same Fourier framework, new geometry

The Bayesian Fourier-mode analysis developed for M31 (Chapter 3) is applied to IC 342's very different geometry — a near face-on, barred spiral — modelling the azimuthal variation of polarisation angle in each radial ring as a superposition of Fourier modes.

02

Bayesian model selection, ring by ring

Rather than assuming a fixed set of Fourier modes everywhere, Bayesian model selection determines — independently for each radial ring — exactly which combination of modes is actually required to explain the data.

03

Handling a poorly constrained foreground

In the central region, the foreground Galactic rotation measure was found to be poorly constrained and strongly correlated with other parameters. Fixing it to the average value from the outer rings removed these unphysical correlations and yielded well-constrained fits.

Results

A classic axisymmetric field — with a bar-driven twist at the centre

"The regular magnetic field of IC 342 is dominated by the axisymmetric mode across almost the entire radial range studied — confirming its original identification as a clean example of an axisymmetric spiral field."
  • The field is axisymmetric (m = 0) across most of the disk, but an additional bisymmetric (m = 1) mode is required in the two outermost rings (7.5–17.5 kpc), and a quadrisymmetric (m = 2) mode appears in the innermost ring, coincident with the nuclear bar.
  • The magnetic pitch angle decreases steadily with radius across the outer disk — from −29° at 2.5–7.5 kpc to −18° at 12.5–17.5 kpc — in good agreement with earlier single-frequency estimates.
  • The innermost ring shows a remarkably steeper pitch angle (−45°), suggesting the dynamo's α-effect dominates locally over the Ω-effect in the bar region, likely driven by non-circular gas flows.
  • Converting fitted amplitudes to physical units gives regular field strengths of order a few μG throughout the disk, comparable in the nuclear bar and outer disk, but notably weaker and less well constrained in the 2.5–7.5 kpc ring.
Why it matters

A second galaxy, the same methodology

IC 342 is the second galaxy analysed with the Bayesian Fourier-mode framework introduced for M31, and the two results together show the method holds up across very different galactic environments — from M31's gentle axisymmetric spiral to IC 342's barred, near face-on disk. Comparing the two builds toward a statistically consistent picture of how magnetic fields are structured across nearby spiral galaxies more broadly.

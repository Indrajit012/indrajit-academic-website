The fine structure of the mean magnetic field in M31

Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 545(2), staf2041 (2025)

Overview

Reading a galaxy's magnetism from its polarised light

Magnetic fields are woven through the Universe at every scale — from the Earth's core to the diffuse gas between galaxies. On galactic scales, spiral galaxies like the Milky Way and M31 host coherent magnetic fields of a few microgauss, threading tens of kiloparsecs across their disks. Though far weaker than the fields around stars or planets, their energy is comparable to that of turbulent gas and cosmic rays — making them a genuinely dynamical ingredient of a galaxy's structure, not just a passive trace.

This paper focuses on M31, the Andromeda Galaxy — the nearest large spiral to our own, and the closest well-resolved laboratory we have for testing how these galactic-scale magnetic fields are generated and shaped. We revisit its magnetic field using a Bayesian Monte Carlo fitting approach, extending the classical Fourier-mode picture of the field to higher order and placing it on a statistically rigorous footing for the first time.

M31 is the largest member of the Local Group — an SA(s)b-type spiral with over a trillion stars, extensively mapped across the electromagnetic spectrum. Its size, proximity, and well-characterised regular magnetic field make it a long-standing benchmark for testing galactic dynamo theory.

Observational data

Four wavelengths, one field

The analysis combines radio continuum polarisation observations from the Effelsberg 100-m telescope and archival VLA data, spanning four wavelengths to disentangle the magnetic field geometry from Faraday rotation and depolarisation effects.

Method

A Bayesian approach to an old picture

01

Fourier-mode decomposition

The regular magnetic field is expressed as a sum of Fourier modes (m = 0, 1, 2, 3...) in galactocentric azimuth — the standard language for describing dynamo-generated fields, where the axisymmetric m = 0 mode represents the dominant spiral pattern.

02

MCMC fitting across four radial rings

Rather than a single χ²-based fit, a Bayesian Monte Carlo procedure is applied independently to four radial rings (6–8, 8–10, 10–12, 12–14 kpc), returning full posterior distributions and honest parameter uncertainties instead of single best-fit values.

03

Modelling Faraday rotation and depolarisation

Two phenomenological depolarisation parameters account for the effective loss of polarised signal at longer wavelengths, avoiding uncertain assumptions about the turbulent magneto-ionic medium while still reproducing the observed polarisation angles.

Results

A predominantly axisymmetric field, with fine structure on top

"The regular magnetic field of M31 is predominantly axisymmetric, with a coherent trailing spiral geometry consistent with mean-field dynamo theory."
  • MCMC chains converge to unique, well-defined solutions for every radial ring (Gelman–Rubin R − 1 < 0.01), with no significant degeneracies between parameters.
  • Normalised residuals between observed and modelled polarisation angles stay close to 1σ or better across all four rings (RMS values of 1.10, 0.99, 0.87, 0.81), showing the model captures the dominant field structure well.
  • Higher-order Fourier modes (m = 1–4) are needed to reproduce localised departures from axial symmetry — but remain comparatively weak, likely tracing local perturbations rather than independent dynamo modes.
  • The magnetic field strength inferred independently from Faraday rotation measures is consistent with recent literature, offering a more direct estimate of the coherent field than polarised-intensity methods alone.
Why it matters

A framework beyond M31

Beyond the specific result for M31, this work establishes a general Bayesian methodology for inferring the regular magnetic field orientation, pitch angle, and strength of nearby spiral galaxies directly from multi-frequency polarisation data — while simultaneously accounting for Faraday rotation and depolarisation. The same framework is applied next to IC 342, providing a statistically consistent basis for comparing magnetic field properties across different galactic environments.
